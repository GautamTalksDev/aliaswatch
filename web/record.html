How to read this

Each tick is one day. Its height is how far that day sat from the model's own trailing baseline. A short tick is ordinary variation - the same prompt at temperature 0 does not return the same answer twice, and that noise is measured rather than assumed.

Hover or focus a trace and step through it with the arrow keys to see any individual day. Open a model for its per-family breakdown. Filter to a single family to see whether a change was concentrated or broad.

A day is called changed only when a family of tasks moves beyond its floor on two days within three, after a false-discovery correction across the six families. One loud day is published as an excursion and nothing more. The methodology gives the measured false-alarm rate, established before any flag was ever published.

Check the record yourself

Each day is hashed and chained to the day before, and the resulting head is signed. Rewriting any past day breaks every link after it. This recomputes the whole chain in your browser - nothing is sent anywhere.

AliasWatch measures the alias, not the weights. Routing, system-prompt, quantisation and weight changes all land here identically and cannot be told apart. AliasWatch reports that the thing you call by that name behaves differently. It never asserts that a provider swapped a model.
